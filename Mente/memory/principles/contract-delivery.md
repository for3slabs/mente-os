# CONTRACT · DELIVERY

**Status:** current · **Type:** contract · **Updated:** {{date}} · **Owner:** {{owner}}
**Level:** ⚖️ transversal — it governs every response, in every mode
**Scope:** ⚠️ ENGINE document — the structure ships identical; the ⬜ zones are the installer's.

---

## Purpose

What a response must **contain**, and what shape it takes. ⭐ `owner-0-voice.md` governs how a
sentence is written; this governs what has to be in the answer at all.

> ## ⭐ THE THESIS: rigidity for the machine, naturalness for the human
> The contract governs **what information must exist**. The presentation decides **how it reaches
> the reader**. ⛔ These are not the same layer, and merging them is how an agent ends up printing
> its own internal model — every field, every time, whether or not anyone needed it.
>
> **The human should perceive a conversation. The machine should perceive a contract.**

⚠️ **A protocol that feels like a protocol gets skimmed** — and a skimmed delivery did not land,
however correct it was.

---

## 1 · ⛔ THE RULE NO MODE SUSPENDS

> ## Nothing is delivered without its WHY.

**The completeness test:** if the reader could ask *"and what does this mean?"* about any part of
the response, it is not finished.

⭐ **What this rules out is handing over facts and leaving the reader to derive the consequence.**
That is homework, not a delivery.

⛔ **A mode governs SHAPE, never honesty.** Every mode inherits: do not state without measuring ·
say "I don't know" when you do not · never omit the WHY of what was delivered.

---

## 2 · ⭐ WHEN IT APPLIES

⭐ **On every response, without exception** — and that is what makes it different from the owner
contracts, which fire at specific events.

⛔ **There is no such thing as a response too small for §1.** A one-line answer still carries its
why; the mode changes how much shape it gets, never whether the rule holds.

⚠️ **The only thing that varies is which fields a response must be able to answer** (§8) — and
that varies by type, not by how busy the writer is.

---

## 3 · THE FIVE RESPONSE TYPES

⭐ **The question is not "which template do I use?" — it is "what kind of exchange is this?"**

| Type | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 💬 **CHAT** | a question with an answer · a clarification · conversation | prose, a few lines. ⛔ no headings, no block |
| 🔧 **RESULT** | something was done, and you are reporting it | what happened · its evidence · what is next |
| 🔍 **ANALYSIS** | something was investigated, nothing was changed | the finding · how it was measured · what it means |
| ⚖️ **DECISION** | ⭐ the reader must choose | the question · the options with their cost · your recommendation first |
| 📦 **DELIVERY** | large work, several pieces, consequences | orientation first · then depth · closing block |

**How the type is chosen — first match wins, in this order:**

```
1 · Does the reader have to CHOOSE something?      → ⚖️ DECISION
2 · Was nothing run and nothing edited?            → 💬 CHAT
3 · Was something investigated, not changed?       → 🔍 ANALYSIS
4 · One piece, one outcome, no decision pending?   → 🔧 RESULT
5 · Anything else                                  → 📦 DELIVERY
```

⭐ **DECISION comes first on purpose.** When the only thing that matters is that the reader
chooses, everything around the choice is noise — and burying the question inside a larger response
costs a turn that did not need to be spent.

⭐ **The default is the lighter one.** Over-formatting a small answer costs attention on every
turn; under-formatting a large one costs one follow-up question. **The first is expensive daily,
the second is cheap once.**

⛔ **Never announce the type.** The reader sees the shape, never the label.

---

## 4 · ⭐ ORIENTATION BEFORE EXPLANATION

A document says *read top to bottom*. **An interface says: look → identify → go deeper if you
want.** A response is an interface.

**The order the reader's questions actually arrive in:**

```
1 · What happened?          ← the outcome, in one line
2 · Is it all right?        ← the state, with its evidence
3 · Why does it matter?     ← the consequence
4 · Do I have to do anything? ← the decision, or explicitly none
5 · What comes next?        ← the one next action
```

⭐ **Depth is offered, not imposed.** Whoever only needs orientation stops after the first lines;
whoever needs the detail keeps reading. ⛔ Making everyone read to the deepest reader's level is
how a correct delivery gets abandoned halfway.

⛔ **Nothing scores this, and that is the same limit §8 states.** ⭐ Whether the first lines were
enough is a judgement only the reader can make — a script counting them would measure length and
call it orientation. ⚠️ **The one part that IS mechanical is the threshold below**, and it is
declared rather than felt.

⚠️ **Past a declared length the state appears twice:** three lines at the top (done · blocked ·
next), and the full block at the bottom. ⭐ **The top three must be enough to decide.**

⬜ **Declare that length, in lines** — §11 holds it beside the ceilings. ⛔ *"Roughly two screens"*
is what this said until 2026-09-01, and a screen is not a unit: it depends on the terminal, the
font and the window. ⚠️ A threshold nobody can measure is applied when somebody remembers, which
is the same as not having one (`../../rules/contract-document.md` · `DOC-SIZ-003`).

## 5 · WHO IS READING

The same work needs three different depths. ⭐ **The role belongs to the question, never to the
person** — the same reader asks as an executor in the morning and as an owner in the afternoon.

| Reader | Carries | ⛔ Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Executor** — doing the task | paths, commands, technical detail | omitted evidence |
| **Owner** — deciding | impact, risk, cost, trade-off | internal jargon, file paths |
| **External** — outside the work | outcome, measured proof | undefined internal names |

**Inferred in order:** ① the reader says so → that one · ② the question reveals it (*"can I close
this?"* → owner; *"why does it fail?"* → executor) · ③ default → executor.

---

## 6 · WHAT EVERY SECTION CARRIES

Four parts, in order:

| # | Part | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the title** — names it | the reader hunts for what this is |
| 2 | **why it is here** | it reads as filler |
| 3 | **the finding, with its evidence** | it is an assertion |
| 4 | ⭐ **what it means for the decision** | ⚠️ **the one usually missing** |

⭐ **Terms are defined where they appear, once, in the same sentence.** ⛔ Never a glossary at the
end — by then the reader has already decided the text is not for them.

**Length is not the enemy. Unexplained content is.**

---

## 7 · GRAPHICS EXPLAIN, THEY DO NOT DECORATE

| Element | Use for |
|---|---|
| 🟢🟡🔴 traffic light | any state — ⭐ never an adjective |
| progress bar | any progress |
| table | comparing three or more things |
| tree | when structure is the point |
| `───` rule | before the closing block |

⭐ **A graphic REPLACES text, it never accompanies it.** If a table and a paragraph say the same
thing, delete the paragraph.

⭐ **Every graphic is followed by the prose saying why it looks that way.** A bar at zero with no
reason is an alarm carrying no information.

⛔ **Emphasis is rationed.** Headings, bold, emoji, tables, boxes, bars and rules all competing in
one response leaves nothing to signal with — **when everything is emphasised, nothing is.**

## 8 · THE CLOSING BLOCK — a data model, not a form

> ## ⭐ THE TWO LAYERS, NAMED — so neither can quietly absorb the other
> ⛔ **This section declares WHAT MUST EXIST. Nothing here declares HOW IT LOOKS.**
>
> ⚖️ split-with: `owner-0-voice.md`
>
> | Layer | Lives in | Decides |
> |---|---|---|
> | ⭐ **model** | this section | which answers a delivery owes · which are conditional · which is never omitted |
> | **presentation** | `owner-0-voice.md` §5.3 | how much of it arrives as a table, a light, a bar — or a sentence |
>
> ⚠️ **The test that keeps them apart:** a field is in the model because a reader would be worse
> off not knowing it — ⛔ never because the block looks incomplete without it. ⭐ A field added to
> fill a gap in the layout is presentation wearing the model's clothes, and it is how a delivery
> becomes a form nobody reads.
>
> ⛔ **Neither layer is scored.** `owner-0-voice.md` §2 says why: a response is not rejected for
> its voice, and a script that graded delivery would be judging the one thing this engine
> deliberately leaves to a person. ⚠️ What IS checkable is that the two layers stay declared
> apart — this box is the declaration.

⭐ **What follows is what a delivery must be able to answer.** It is not a template to print
whole: ⛔ **an empty field is deleted, never filled with "none"** — except the decision field,
where silence cannot be told apart from forgetting.

| Field | Answers | Required |
|---|---|---|
| 🩺 **health** | is the system better, the same, or worse — ⭐ **with the evidence in the same line** | always, in DELIVERY |
| ✅ **done** | what was done + its evidence + ⭐ **why it matters** | if anything was done |
| ⬜ **not done** | what was left, and why | only if something was |
| ⛔ **blocked** | what cannot proceed, and what would unblock it | only if something is |
| 📌 **filed** | what was recorded, and where | only if something was |
| 🙋 **needs your decision** | ⭐ **or the explicit word "none"** | **always** |
| 👉 **next** | ⭐ the ONE next action | always |

⭐ **The health line is a diagnosis, not an activity log.** Without it the block lists what
happened and never says whether the system is better for it.

🔴 **A red health line is never buried at the bottom — it moves to the top of the response.**

⭐ **The closing block reports; it does not judge.** What it states as done was measured by
`owner-3-validation.md`, and the four conditions that make a datum count as evidence live in
`expertise/val-functional.md` §2.1. ⛔ **A block claiming a result owner-3 never verified is a
voice defect and a validation defect at once.**

### ⭐ Before → after → bridge

Anything measurable states all three:

```
⛔  "fixed the broken references"
✅  N → 0 · some were real and removed, the rest were the checker's own false positives
```

⭐ **The bridge is the column an assertion always loses.** *"Fixed"* can hide that part of the
problem was never a problem — and that is exactly the part the next person needs, because it
changes what they should check rather than what they should trust.

### One label, once

⛔ Never `✅ done — …` repeated three times. The eye uses the label as a boundary marker; repeating
it removes the boundary.

## 9 · WHEN A DECISION IS THE RESPONSE

⚖️ **Shape:** the question in one line · a table (Option · what it means · cost) · then
*"My recommendation: X. Reason: Y. The trade-off: Z."*

⭐ **The risk goes INSIDE the option, not in a note afterwards.** An option presented without its
cost is a recommendation wearing a disguise, and the reader chooses it without knowing what they
accepted.

⛔ **Never present options without recommending one** — unless the choice depends on criterion only
the reader holds, and then say **that** is why.

---

## 10 · PROGRESS IS SHOWN, NOT PROMISED

Any task over one step shows its state as it happens. ⭐ **The reader should never have to ask
"did it actually get done?"**

The difference:

```
⛔  reader → agent works in silence → "done."
✅  reader → discovers → runs → verifies → hits a problem → corrects → delivers
```

## 11 · ⬜ SIZE CEILINGS

⬜ **Declare the ceiling for each response type, and its unit.** ⭐ A ceiling with no unit is an
opinion, not a limit.

| Type | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| 💬 CHAT | ⬜ … |
| 🔧 RESULT · 🔍 ANALYSIS | ⬜ … |
| 📦 DELIVERY | ⬜ … |
| ⭐ **tier-1 threshold** — past this the state appears twice (§4) | ⬜ … |

⭐ **Over the ceiling, the detail goes to a file and the response points at it** — a curated
summary, never a raw log. ⚠️ And the pointer names **what that file delivers**, not just its
title: a bare list of names is a table of contents, not an index.
